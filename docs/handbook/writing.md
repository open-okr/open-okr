# Writing objectives and key results

Twenty-six checks run as you type: five on objectives, seven on key results,
and the rest on cycles and alignment. They are advisory by default and
strictness is an administrator's setting, but the publish gates are not
advisory: a set does not go out until the key results pass.

Every message cites the rule behind it, so you can open the rule and argue with
it. Nothing is a mysterious red dot.

## Objectives: a destination, not a delivery

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Launch the new mobile app by end of Q3 | Make mobile the way our customers prefer to reach us |

Launch is an output. You can launch and still fail. The strong version names
the change in customer behaviour, and the launch becomes a means to it.

The test: **if we do this and nothing changes, did we succeed?** If yes, it is
not an objective.

What the checks look for:

- An output verb at the front: launch, build, ship, deliver, roll out. A
  deliverable is not a destination.
- No measure anywhere, so nobody can tell whether it happened.
- Length. An objective nobody can repeat from memory is one nobody acts on.
- More than the cap: five company objectives, three per unit. More than that is
  a list of work rather than a set of priorities.

## Key results: from, to, and how you will know

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Improve customer satisfaction | Increase NPS from 32 to 50 (lagging). Cut first-response time from 9h to 2h (leading) |

No baseline, no target, no way to score it. The strong pair sets **from** and
**to**, and combines a lagging proof with a leading signal you can steer
weekly.

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Hold 12 customer interviews | Raise activation rate of new sign-ups from 41% to 60% |

Interviews are activity. Ask what the interviews are *for*, and measure that.

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Increase sales calls from 40 to 120 per week | Grow qualified pipeline from $1.2M to $3.0M (lagging). Lift call-to-meeting conversion from 8% to 15% (leading) |

This one is measurable and still an output. If 120 calls create no pipeline,
the key result was achieved and the quarter was wasted. That is the trap
measurability alone does not catch.

## Two to five per objective

One key result is a measure pretending to be an objective. More than five is a
plan, and a plan does not focus anybody.

Pair them where you can: one lagging result that proves the outcome, one
leading indicator you can move this week. An objective with only lagging
measures gives the weekly session nothing to talk about until it is too late.

## Confidence at drafting

Above 0.9 is sandbagging and the Coach says so: a goal everybody is already
confident about was not worth setting as an OKR. Below 0.25 is ambitious, which
is allowed, and flagged so that it is a choice rather than an accident.

## Alignment: contribute, do not copy

A team objective that restates the company objective aligns nothing. State what
this goal **contributes to** and how.

Horizontal alignment is a dependency both teams know about, which is why a
dependency has to be confirmed by the other side or logged with a named risk
owner before the set can publish. An unconfirmed dependency is a plan built on
somebody else's guess.

## What the Coach does with all this

It flags as you type, and it runs a nightly semantic sweep for what structure
alone cannot see: two teams measuring the same thing in different words, a goal
that belongs under a different parent, a dependency nobody declared.

It proposes. A person approves. That is the default and changing it is an
explicit, per-agent decision.
