# The weekly rhythm

Fifteen to thirty minutes, once a week, in four steps. This is the loop that
makes OKRs work, and skipping it is the single commonest way the practice dies.

It is a **decision loop**, not a status meeting. Nobody reads out what they did.

## The four steps

**1. Confidence round.** Every key result gets a confidence score from
everybody. Votes reveal together, so nobody anchors on what the champion said
first, and the champion writes what actually changed this week.

**2. Discuss what dropped.** Any key result whose confidence fell gets a short
discussion. Every low score gets a next action and its owner before the session
moves past it, due by the next check-in. Raise a blocker too only where
something is actually blocked: a next step is not a blocker.

**3. Commitments and wins.** Close last week's, delivered or not, with a line
on why where it helps. Then set three or four for this week, each with an owner
and a linked key result: the few moves that shift a key result, not a to-do
list. Then name the week's wins.

**4. Digest.** The product assembles it, with the open blockers and their next
actions, the commitments and the wins, and any of the space's goals that have
gone stale. Once the session closes, the coordinator adds a note for leadership
and posts it to the team's Slack or Teams channel, when the space has linked
one in its settings.

The session ends with a streak. That is deliberate: the thing worth making
visible is that the team kept the rhythm, not that the numbers were good. It
counts your space's own check-in periods: weeks, or fortnights or months if
your space checks in every two weeks or monthly, so a team that chose every two
weeks does not break its streak in the week between.

Mark your space's holidays on the space home. No check-in is due in a holiday,
nobody is nudged about the space on one, and the streak does not break. A week
counts as a holiday when its last working day is inside one, so a team back by
the Friday still checks in that week. A goal that was due in a week you mark
moves on to the week after it, on the same weekday.

## Confidence, and what the numbers mean

| Score | What it means in the session |
|---|---|
| Above 0.7 | On track. Move on |
| 0.4 to 0.7 | Watch. The champion says what changed |
| Below 0.4 | The session's business. It needs discussion |
| Below 0.3 | It needs a decision, not a discussion |

Confidence that never moves is the failure this step exists to catch. A key
result at 0.8 for nine weeks and then 0.2 in week ten was never at 0.8.

## Blockers

A low score becomes a typed blocker. Five types, and the type is the point:
naming it is what makes it somebody's to fix rather than everybody's to worry
about.

| Type | It means |
|---|---|
| **Resource** | No capacity, budget or tools to progress the key result |
| **Dependency** | Progress waits on another team's output or decision |
| **Clarity** | The key result is ambiguous. Nobody agrees what done means |
| **Priority conflict** | Business as usual keeps displacing OKR work |
| **External** | Market, regulation or partner factors beyond your control |
| **Approach not working** | The work is happening and the number is not moving |
| **Other** | Anything else, described in a line |

Every blocker gets an owner and a next action due by the next check-in. The
owner is reminded the day before; if the check-in passes with the action still
open, the coordinator hears about it, visibly rather than politely.

**A clarity blocker is usually a writing problem.** If nobody agrees what done
means, the key result is the thing to fix, not the work. See
[writing](writing.md).

## Between sessions

The product runs the cadence rather than reminding you to.

| What happens | When |
|---|---|
| A check-in is due | The champion is nudged before it, not after |
| A check-in is missed | The escalation ladder starts: the champion again, the reviewer where the goal has one, then the coordinator. The sponsor only where your workspace puts the sponsor in its ladders |
| A key result's confidence falls into the low band | The coordinator hears the same day. One drafted low on purpose that stays there does not escalate. At 0.3 and below the sponsor hears too, only where your workspace turns critical escalation on |
| A goal goes unchecked | After three days of grace it reads as **outdated**, whatever health was last reported |
| A blocker's clock runs low | Its owner hears, then the coordinator |

Every message says which rule sent it, on which channel, and where it sits on
the ladder. A snooze quietens the message and never hides the obligation: the
review inbox still says you owe it.

## Monthly

Thirty to sixty minutes: trend per objective, the dependency and risk log,
resource shifts, continue, update, start or stop, and decisions recorded. It
exists because some problems are only visible over four weeks, and because a
decision nobody wrote down gets made again next month.

The review lists what was started, updated or stopped since the plan was
published. Continue needs no record. Update a target on its objective, where
easing one asks for a reason. Stop an objective from the review itself, with a
line on why it no longer matters: it closes as abandoned and the review names
it.
