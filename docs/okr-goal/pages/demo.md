## 2. How it works

| 1. Open it | 2. Pick a person | 3. Look around |
|---|---|---|
| The demo runs the same release as OKRGoal, the latest one | The sign-in screen lists the people at Northwind. Click one and sign in as them | Change things, check in, draft a bad objective. Everything goes back the way it was overnight |

**Line:** The story is placed on today's calendar, so the demo moves through Northwind's year as the real year moves. What you see in March is not what you see in September.

> **Decision needed:** the demo has an AI provider connected within a small daily budget. Choose whether to say so: *"AI is on, within a daily cap. If the cap runs out, you see exactly what the product does with AI off."*

## 4. Six things to try

- **Write a bad objective.** Start one with "Launch" and give it a key result like "Hold 12 customer interviews". Watch the Coach.
- **Open Review.** It is the page that says what this person owes, overdue first.
- **Open the Work Map.** The whole company on one tree. Find the goal marked outdated.
- **Open the recovery board.** Every unhealthy KPI and what is being done about it.
- **Read a quarterly review's minutes.** Then find the diagnostic: strategy or rhythm?
- **Click a rule.** Any coaching message. It opens the rule it came from.

## 5. The story behind it

## A year of OKRs, step by step.

The demo is built from a written scenario: one company's full year in OKRGoal, from a pilot to the year-end. Every step names the screen and the practice rule it shows.

| Q1: the rollout | Q2: the competitor | Q3: the long summer | Q4: the year-end |
|---|---|---|---|
| The first company-wide cycle. Drafting with the Coach, publishing through the gates, a KPI that drops, the first close | A rival launches in week 6. Objectives stopped and started, a committed key result at risk, a leaver | Holidays, a recovery OKR for operating margin, a reorganisation, a leader on leave, a new team | Renewal season, an incident, planning the next year, the annual review and the scorecard |
