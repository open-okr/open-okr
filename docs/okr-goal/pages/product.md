## 2. Six stages of a quarter

### Stage 1. Plan

**Image:** cycle workspace ([02-cycle-workspace.png](../../mockups/png/02-cycle-workspace.png))

## Start from evidence, not opinion.

A cycle opens with a guided path: name a sponsor and a facilitator, book the sessions, and gather the input pack. Score last quarter. Read the KPI baselines. Rank the strategic issues. Choose three to five priorities and write down what will be measurably different in twelve months. Then write the not-doing list, which most organisations skip and OKRGoal does not let you.

| The product does | You do |
|---|---|
| Knows which phase you are in, what is missing and how many weeks remain before the publication date | Decide what matters this quarter |
| Feeds in last cycle's scores, learnings and carry-forward items | Decide what to stop |
| Shows the missing input pack items beside every draft form | Bring the evidence |

**Small print:** By default the phases guide and never lock anybody out. A workspace that runs a formal planning process can make them binding. One that only wants to track OKRs can hide them.

### Stage 2. Draft

**Image:** draft coach ([03-draft-coach.png](../../mockups/png/03-draft-coach.png)) and the rule card ([03b-rule-card.png](../../mockups/png/03b-rule-card.png))

## Write it once. Write it well.

Every objective and key result is checked as you type, against twenty-six quality checks. Each check gives a verdict, a coaching prompt, the reason it matters and a weak-versus-strong example. Your set carries a strength score that moves with every keystroke.

| The product does | You do |
|---|---|
| Flags the deliverable dressed as an objective, the activity dressed as a key result, the target with no baseline, the confidence that is too high to be a stretch | Decide what to change |
| With an AI provider on: rewrites a failing line to pass the rule it broke, and suggests metrics, targets and the parent it aligns to | Accept, edit or dismiss |

**Pull quote:** "Every verdict opens into the rule itself. Coaching is arguable by design."

### Stage 3. Commit

**Image:** gates and capacity ([04-gates-capacity.png](../../mockups/png/04-gates-capacity.png)) and alignment ([05-alignment-studio.png](../../mockups/png/05-alignment-studio.png))

## Align by contribution. Publish through the gates.

Map how each goal contributes to the one above it. Register the dependencies between teams, and confirm them with the team you depend on. Check capacity against the initiatives you actually planned, and record what you cut. Then publish through six gates.

| Gate | What it checks | Default |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Every objective has a title and a named champion, and a reviewer where reviewers are required | Block |
| 2 | Every objective has a key result, and every key result passes the checks set to block | Block |
| 3 | Every objective states what it contributes to, or why it stands alone | Warn |
| 4 | Every dependency is confirmed, escalated, or logged with a named risk owner | Warn |
| 5 | No committed OKR is left marked as exceeding capacity | Warn |
| 6 | A publication date is set | Off |

**Line under the table:** Each gate can block, warn or be off, and an admin can override a blocking gate with a recorded reason. A plan where nothing was cut is a plan that has not been made.

### Stage 4. Run the rhythm

**Image:** weekly session ([07-weekly-session.png](../../mockups/png/07-weekly-session.png)) and review inbox ([10-review-inbox.png](../../mockups/png/10-review-inbox.png))

## Fifteen minutes a week, run by the product.

The weekly check-in session has four steps. A confidence round, private and revealed together so nobody anchors on the loudest person. A short discussion of what dropped, with a next action for every low score. Commitments and wins. A digest, written for you and posted to the team channel. Then the streak ticks up.

Between sessions, the Review page tells each person what they owe, overdue first. The OKR Champion reminds, then escalates, always visibly. Every blocker has an owner and is due by the next check-in.

| The product does | You do |
|---|---|
| Reminds, escalates, opens the session, writes the digest, keeps the streak | Say honestly how confident you are |
| Drafts the overdue check-in from what actually moved | Name what is blocking you |
| Marks a goal **outdated** when nobody checked in, whatever it last claimed | Decide the next move |

### Stage 5. Watch the numbers

**Image:** KPI recovery ([06-kpi-recovery.png](../../mockups/png/06-kpi-recovery.png))

## When a KPI drops, the fix is already drafted.

KPIs describe the health of the business. OKRs describe what you are changing about it. OKRGoal holds both, in one tree. Build KPI driver trees down to the leading indicators a team can move this week. Give each KPI a health band. When one turns unhealthy, choose a response: fix it as day-to-day work, add a key result to an existing objective, or launch a recovery OKR the Champion has already drafted from the leading drivers that are short.

### Stage 6. Close and learn

**Image:** quarterly review ([08-quarterly-review.png](../../mockups/png/08-quarterly-review.png))

## Ninety minutes. Honest scores. A real diagnosis.

The quarterly review runs in four acts and eleven timed stages, about two weeks before the cycle ends. Open the room before the numbers. Score every key result against evidence, with the objective score hidden until the team reveals it together. Hear the story behind each number. Then the retro, the root causes, and an anonymous check on the process itself.

Then the diagnostic, which is the most useful thing the session produces:

| What happened | What it means |
|---|---|
| You hit the results | The question is not effort. It is whether the ambition was high enough to be worth the quarter |
| You missed, and the rhythm was strong | A strategy or quality problem. Fix the key results, not the people |
| You missed, and the rhythm was weak | A rhythm problem. Restore the weekly check-in before you rewrite a single objective |

Every objective is closed deliberately: keep, modify or abandon, with a reason. Nothing carries over by default. Scores, learnings and the weakest part of how you ran the quarter feed the next cycle's planning automatically.

## 3. The screens you live in

## Five places, and you will use three of them every week.

| Card | Copy |
|---|---|
| **The Work Map** | The home screen. One company-wide tree of goals, key results, initiatives and KPIs, with health, progress, confidence, owner and next step at every level, live. |
| **Review** | What you owe right now. Check-ins due, acknowledgements owed, blockers you own, commitments due, sessions to run and agent proposals waiting for you. Overdue first. |
| **Spaces** | Team homes, each with its own goals, sessions, documents and members. |
| **The work** | Initiatives that move a key result, a board where every card can link to one, and documents with version history. Enough to answer "what is moving this key result?". Not a project-management suite. |
| **The copilot** | A side panel that answers questions about your workspace, with citations, and proposes actions for you to confirm. Only shown when an AI provider is connected. |

## 5. What it is not

## Deliberately OKR-shaped.

We would rather do OKRs properly than ten things loosely. So OKRGoal does not have, and does not plan for its first version:

- Gantt charts and dependency scheduling
- Time and cost tracking
- Sprints, backlogs and story points
- Custom fields and custom workflows
- Native mobile apps. The web app works on a phone, and so do Slack, Teams, WhatsApp and Telegram.

If you need those, keep your project tool, and link its work to a key result.
