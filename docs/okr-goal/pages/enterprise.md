## 2. The problem

**Eyebrow:** The problem

## Enterprise OKR programmes fail the same way. Just more expensively.

**Three cards:**

| The rollout stalls | Every department invents its own | Leadership finds out at the close |
|---|---|---|
| The first quarter has a consultant, workshops and energy. By the second, check-ins are optional and the tool is a filing cabinet. | One division writes tasks as key results, another writes ten objectives, a third skips the review. Nobody can compare anything. | The quarter ends, the numbers are missed, and there is no way to tell whether the strategy was wrong or the work never happened. |

## 3. The practice

**Eyebrow:** The practice

## One practice. Every department. Your rules.

Choose a profile for the whole organisation, then tune it. The **Governed** profile makes the planning phases binding, sets a planning window for writing OKRs, routes new mid-cycle objectives through the reviewer, requires a reviewer on every goal, and blocks on five of the six publish gates. Every rule you change is audited, and a closed cycle keeps the rules it was graded under.

**Four cards:**

| Card | Copy |
|---|---|
| **A guided cycle, organisation-wide** | Annual and quarterly cycles, a sponsor and facilitator per cycle, a countdown to the publication date, and a phase checklist computed from the work. |
| **Quality at the source** | Twenty-six checks on every line, in every department, at the moment of writing. Publish gates that hold the line before the quarter starts. |
| **Alignment that reaches the top** | Company, department and team levels. A score that names every orphan goal and every department with no cross-team dependency. |
| **A close that tells you what to fix** | The diagnostic separates strategy problems from rhythm problems, space by space, from real check-ins. |

## 4. Governance

**Eyebrow:** Governance

## Control without a committee.

| Card | Copy |
|---|---|
| **Spaces with their own perimeter** | Every team gets a space with its own goals, sessions, documents and members, and its own access scope. |
| **Roles you can edit** | Four built-in roles, Owner, Admin, Member and Viewer, each granting a level per domain. Edit them, or add your own. |
| **Access that is checked once, everywhere** | One permission check serves the browser, the API, the command line, the chat apps and every agent. There are no back routes. |
| **Isolation in the database** | Every workspace is isolated by row-level security inside PostgreSQL. A bug in the application cannot read across workspaces. |
| **An audit log you can verify** | Append-only and hash-chained, so a changed or deleted row shows. Verify the whole chain from the admin screen. |
| **Freeze for maintenance** | Put the workspace into read-only for a cutover or an incident, with a named list of admins who can still act. |

## 5. AI under control

**Eyebrow:** AI under control

## AI your security team can sign off.

| You decide | And it is enforced |
|---|---|
| Which provider, and whose key: the instance's, the workspace's, or each person's | Every call is metered by person, by feature, by agent and by model |
| Which features are on, one switch each | Quotas per person, per agent and per workspace, and a hard cap that halts a run |
| How much context may leave: everything, assists only, or nothing | Email addresses and phone numbers redacted before anything leaves |
| Which hosts it may call | An allow-list, and a refused call is an audit row |
| Whether agents may write, or only propose | Proposals wait in a review queue. Sandbox mode commits nothing |
| A local model, or none at all | With a local model, nothing leaves your network. With none, the practice still runs |
