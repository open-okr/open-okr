Every OKRGoal workspace comes with two AI members. The **OKR Coach** guards the quality of your OKRs. The **OKR Champion** guards the rhythm that keeps them alive. They start the conversation, in the browser and in your chat apps, and every message tells you which rule it comes from.

## 2. Members, not features

## They have names, a scope and a job.

The Coach and the Champion are members of your workspace. They appear in the activity feed. You can mention them on a goal. Their actions are in the audit log under their own names. And they never count as seats, on any plan.

**Three facts in a row:**

| Seeded in every workspace | Scoped to what you give them | Never a seat |
|---|---|---|
| Both are there the moment the workspace exists. Nothing to install. | Each sees only the spaces, goals and KPI trees it is bound to. Never the whole workspace. | Agents and guests are free on every plan. |

## 3. The OKR Coach

**Eyebrow:** Guards quality

## The OKR Coach reads every draft so the quarter does not start on a bad one.

Its whole vocabulary is the method. It reviews every objective and key result as it is written, guards the publish gates, and speaks at the moments a good facilitator would.

**When it acts:**

| Continuously | At each phase | Nightly | When asked |
|---|---|---|---|
| On every change to a goal, key result or alignment link | It explains what is blocking the phase, and why | A sweep for duplicates, conflicts and drift across the whole tree | Mention it on a goal, or ask it to review a space |

**Four sample messages, as cards:**

> **OKR Coach** · `quality.no_not_doing`
> Phase 3 is ending and the not-doing list is empty. What are you choosing not to do this year?

> **OKR Coach** · `quality.no_cuts`
> Capacity was checked and nothing was cut. Which initiative makes room for the new ones?

> **OKR Coach** · `quality.divergence`
> *Expand into the Nordics* is reported on track, but none of its key results has moved in a month. Is the status right?

> **OKR Coach** · `quality.sandbagging_close`
> Most of this cycle's aspirational key results scored 1.0. Were the targets a stretch?

## 4. The OKR Champion

**Eyebrow:** Guards the rhythm

## The OKR Champion does the chasing, so nobody has to be the nag.

It reminds the goal's champion before a check-in is due, then escalates when it is late, always where the person can see it. It holds every blocker to the next check-in. It opens the weekly session, writes the digest, keeps the streak, watches every KPI, and prepares the quarterly review so the room starts warm.

**Its week:**

| Every hour | Every morning | Every week | Every cycle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Works out who is due what, on which channel | A summary in each person's own time zone. Checks staleness, blockers and KPIs | Opens and closes the session, writes the digest, updates the streak | Counts down to the publication date, then prepares the review pack |

**Four sample messages, as cards:**

> **OKR Champion** · `checkin.due`
> Your check-in for *Cut time to first value* is due today. What is the number this week? **Check in**

> **OKR Champion** · `blocker.warning`
> The next action on your blocker, *Legal review of the contract template*, is due by tomorrow's check-in.

> **OKR Champion** · `kpi.recovery_proposed`
> *Operating margin* has been unhealthy for two periods. I have drafted a recovery OKR from the three drivers that are short. **Review the draft**

> **OKR Champion** · `digest.weekly`
> Product space, week 7. Confidence 6.8, down from 7.1. Two blockers open, both owned. Four of five commitments delivered. Streak: 7 weeks.

## 5. The escalation ladder

## Escalated, never ambushed.

A missed check-in climbs a ladder, one step at a time, and the person being escalated past can see every step. Nobody is reported behind their back.

**Diagram, five steps left to right:**

| On the day | 1 day late | 3 days late | 7 days late | 14 days late |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The goal's champion | The champion again, on their main channel | The reviewer, if the goal has one. The goal now shows **outdated** | The space coordinator | The sponsor, only if your workspace adds them. Otherwise it stops at the coordinator |

**Line under the diagram:** Every step and every delay is a setting. Holidays pause the clock without breaking the streak, and a member on leave names a delegate.

## 6. Every message cites a rule

## Argue with the rule, not the robot.

Every proactive message is a recorded nudge with four things attached: the rule that sent it, the channel it went to, where it sits on the escalation ladder, and the reason if it was held back. Click the rule and you are reading it in the method.

## 7. Safe by design

## Built for the people who say no to AI.

**Six cards:**

| Card | Copy |
|---|---|
| **They propose. You approve.** | By default every change an agent wants to make becomes a proposal in your Review inbox. Apply it or dismiss it, one at a time or in bulk. |
| **Sandbox mode** | Run an agent in a full dry run. It shows you everything it would do and commits nothing. |
| **Least privilege** | An agent gets explicit access to named spaces, goals and KPI trees. There is no service account with access to everything. |
| **Hard cost caps** | Every step is metered. A cap halts a run mid-flight, with a clear log line. An agent cannot run up an unbounded bill. |
| **Quiet by design** | One nudge per subject per day, unless it escalates. At most five per person per week. The rest wait for the morning summary. |
| **Fully audited** | Every action is in the audit log under the agent's own name, next to who approved it. |

**Line under the cards:** Trust one agent enough? Give it direct writes, scoped to the spaces you name. It is your call, per agent, and it is off until you turn it on.

## 8. With AI and without it

## Turn the AI off. They still work.

The rules behind every nudge, escalation, gate, score and diagnostic are code, not a prompt. Without an AI provider, the agents still do every part of their job that needs a decision. They lose only the drafting and the language.

| | With AI off | With an AI provider |
|---|---|---|
| Quality checks and gates | ✓ All twenty-six checks, all six gates | ✓ Plus a rewrite of each failing line |
| Reminders and escalations | ✓ | ✓ |
| Weekly digest | ✓ From a template | ✓ Written for this week |
| Overdue check-in | A blank check-in, ready to fill | ✓ Drafted from what actually moved |
| Recovery OKR | ✓ Drafted from the drivers that are short | ✓ Plus plain-language reasoning |
| Alignment review | ✓ Structural gaps | ✓ Plus duplicates, better parents and hidden dependencies |
| Closing diagnostic | ✓ The verdict and its prescription | ✓ Plus specifics from this cycle |

**Line under the table:** Every change we make is tested with the AI switched off, so this table stays true.

## 10. FAQ

**Which AI does it use?**
Yours. Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, OpenRouter, Ollama, or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint. Keys can belong to the instance, the workspace or a person. Point it at a local model and nothing leaves your network.

**Can I create more agents?**
Yes. A workspace can create its own agents, each with a persona, instructions, a schedule, a scope and a write policy. The Coach and the Champion are the two that ship with every workspace.

**Will people get spammed?**
No. Nudges are a budget. One per subject per day, at most five per person per week, quiet hours in each person's own time zone, and a snooze on every message. A snooze quietens the message. It never hides the obligation.

**What stops an agent seeing something it should not?**
The same thing that stops a person. Every agent action goes through the same permission check and the same database isolation as a click in the browser, using the agent's own narrow access.

**What data reaches the AI provider?**
What you allow. Admins choose how much context may leave, whether email addresses and phone numbers are redacted, and which hosts may be reached. A local model means nothing leaves at all.
