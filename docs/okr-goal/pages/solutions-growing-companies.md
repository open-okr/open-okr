## 2. The second attempt

## Most companies are on their second try.

The first time, the founders wrote objectives on an offsite. Ten of them. The key results were a to-do list. Check-ins happened for three weeks, then the quarter got busy. By the review, nobody could say what had worked.

None of that was a people problem. Nothing checked the drafts and nothing ran the rhythm. OKRGoal does both.

## 3. Your first quarter, week by week

## What the first quarter looks like.

| When | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Week 0** | Sign up and name the workspace. Every setting has a sensible default, so that is the setup. Invite the leadership team |
| **Week 0** | Draft three company objectives. The Coach flags the one that starts with "launch" and the key result that is really a task. Fix them, or keep them and know why |
| **Week 1** | Teams draft their own, aligned to the company's. The alignment score shows who is not connected to anything |
| **Week 2** | Publish. Connect Slack. The Champion takes it from here |
| **Weeks 3 to 11** | Fifteen minutes on Fridays. Most people check in from Slack in under a minute. The streak climbs. The one person who forgets hears about it, then their coordinator does |
| **Week 6** | A KPI dips. The Champion tells its owner before the board meeting does |
| **Week 11** | Ninety minutes, four acts. Honest scores, and a diagnostic: was a miss strategy or rhythm? |
| **Week 12** | Next quarter's planning starts with this quarter's scores and learnings already in it |

## 4. Start small

## Do less, and do it properly.

Two profiles are built for a first rollout:

| Radical Focus | Lightweight |

In the style of Christina Wodtke's book | Track OKRs without the planning workflow. Check-ins every two weeks. Only the two structural gates. Good for teams who want the habit before the process |

## 7. FAQ

**We are twenty people. Is this too much?**
Choose the Radical Focus or Lightweight profile and it is as small as a shared document, with a coach that does the chasing.

**Do we need an OKR lead?**
No. Somebody should own your OKRs, often the chief of staff or a founder, but the product runs the reminders, the agenda, the digest and the review.

**We have OKRs in a spreadsheet. Can we keep them?**
Upload the spreadsheet. You confirm the column mapping, see a dry run, then import.

**Do we need to use AI?**
No. It drafts and rewrites if you connect it, and every rule runs either way.
