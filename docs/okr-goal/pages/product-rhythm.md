## 2. The rhythm is the product

## 3. A check-in is a narrative, not a dropdown

## Nobody hand-paints a goal green.

A check-in in OKRGoal has four parts, and the goal's health is always derived from the latest one.

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Status** | On track, caution or off track |
| **Confidence** | How likely you are to hit it, out of ten by default |
| **The story** | One or two lines on what changed this week. Facts, not feelings |
| **A snapshot** | Every key result's value at that moment, beside its previous value. It never changes afterwards |

**Line:** Check in when it is due, or any day you have news. With an AI provider connected, the Champion drafts the check-in from what actually moved: value changes, completed linked work and comments since last time. You edit it and publish it.

## 4. The weekly session

## Four steps. Fifteen to thirty minutes.

**Numbered steps, each with a small illustration:**

**1. Confidence round.**
Every key result gets a confidence. Where the team votes, everyone submits privately and the votes reveal together, so nobody anchors on the champion. The champion writes what changed this week.

**2. Discuss what dropped.**
Any key result whose confidence fell gets a short discussion. Every low score leaves with a next action, an owner and a due date: the next check-in. Where something is actually stuck, it becomes a blocker with a type.

**3. Commitments and wins.**
Close last week's commitments, delivered or not. Set this week's: three or four moves that will actually shift a key result. Name the week's wins.

**4. The digest.**
Written for you from the session: open blockers and their next actions, commitments and wins. The coordinator edits it and it goes to your channel.

**Then:** the streak. The number of weeks in a row your team held the session. A skipped week breaks it. A holiday does not. It is a light touch, and it reliably keeps the heartbeat.

**Small print:** Weekly, every two weeks or monthly, per space. The session works in the room, on a call, or across time zones with confidence collected from people who cannot attend.

## 5. Review: what you owe

## Notifications say what happened. Review says what you owe.

One page per person, computed on the server, overdue first.

**Image:** the review inbox ([10-review-inbox.png](../../mockups/png/10-review-inbox.png)).

**Five row types:**

- **Check-ins due**, for goals you champion
- **Acknowledgements owed**, for check-ins from your people. Acknowledge them one by one, or all at once
- **Blockers you own**, with their clocks
- **Commitments due** this week
- **Agent proposals** waiting for your yes or no

**Line:** Every message shows the rule that sent it, the channel it came on and its step on the escalation ladder. A snooze quietens the message. It never hides the obligation.

## 6. Blockers have a clock

## Every blocker has an owner and a deadline.

A blocker in OKRGoal has a type, an owner and a next action, due by the next check-in of the goal it blocks. The Champion reminds the owner the day before. If the check-in passes with the action still open, the coordinator hears about it. It never reopens the discussion. It moves the clock.

**Seven blocker types, as chips:**

`Resource` `Dependency` `Clarity` `Priority conflict` `External` `Approach not working` `Other`

**Tooltip on "Approach not working":** The work is happening and the number is not moving.

## 7. Honest health

## "On track" expires.

Reported health that never changes is the commonest lie in OKR software. In OKRGoal, a goal nobody has checked in on past its grace period is shown as **outdated**, everywhere it appears, whatever its owner last claimed. When reported health and the numbers disagree, the Coach says so.

**Visual:** a goal card showing "On track" crossed out and replaced with an "Outdated · 10 days" badge.

## 8. The monthly review

## Once a month, step back.

The trend for each objective, the dependency and risk log, the resource shifts, and a decision log where every decision names the key result it affects. So in week eleven, when somebody asks why the target moved, the answer is written down.

## 9. The quarterly review

**Image:** the quarterly review at the root-cause stage ([08-quarterly-review.png](../../mockups/png/08-quarterly-review.png)).

## Ninety minutes. Four acts. One honest answer.

About two weeks before the cycle ends. Eleven timed stages, a timer on screen, and minutes you can export at the end. Run it as one session, or as a review and a separate retrospective.

| Act | The question it asks | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Open** | How is the room? | A room pulse before any numbers, because steady rooms round their numbers up |
| **Review** | Did we achieve the results? | Score every key result against evidence. The objective score stays hidden until the team reveals it together. Then the story behind each number, owner by owner. Then recognition |
| **Retro** | How did we work? | What worked and what did not, written silently then dot-voted. The four questions leadership owes the team. One honest cause for every key result under 0.7. An anonymous five-statement survey of the practice itself |
| **Reset** | What do we do next? | Every objective closed as keep, modify or abandon, with a reason. Learnings captured. Every action gets a name and a date |

### The diagnostic

## Strategy problem or rhythm problem?

That is the one question every executive asks at the end of a quarter, and the one no tracker answers. OKRGoal answers it from the cycle's real check-ins.

| What happened | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| You hit the results | The question is not effort | Ask whether the ambition was high enough to be worth the quarter |
| You missed, and the rhythm was strong | A strategy or quality problem | Fix the key results, not the people |
| You missed, and the rhythm was weak | A rhythm problem | Restore the weekly check-in before you rewrite a single objective |

**Then it feeds forward.** Your scores become next cycle's starting point. Unfinished work becomes ranked issues that must earn their place again. Learnings join the input pack. The lowest-scoring statement about the practice becomes next quarter's improvement action. An annual review closes the year the same way.

## 10. Real life

## Built for quarters that do not go to plan.

| Card | Copy |
|---|---|
| **Holidays** | Mark a space's holidays and nobody is nudged. The streak survives |
| **Leave** | A member on leave names a delegate, who covers their check-ins, reviews and acknowledgements. Nobody nudges the person on leave |
| **Change mid-cycle** | Add, stop, re-parent or move an OKR at any point. Every change is visible at the close |
| **Easing a target** | Allowed, with a written reason. The original stays on record |
| **Reorganisations** | When teams merge or split, an objective moves to another space with its key results, check-ins and alignment |
