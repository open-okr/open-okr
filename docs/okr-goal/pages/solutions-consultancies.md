## 2. Week six, again

## You know how this goes.

The workshop was good. The objectives were good. The first two check-ins happened. Then a launch slipped, a manager went on leave, and the practice quietly stopped. When you came back for the quarterly review, half the key results had not been touched in a month.

That is not a failure of your method. It is the gap between your visits, and nothing has filled it until now.

## 3. What it does between your visits

| Card | Copy |
|---|---|
| **Holds the quality bar** | Twenty-six checks on every draft, with the coaching prompt you would have given. The task dressed as a key result does not get past it quietly |
| **Keeps the rhythm** | The Champion reminds, escalates and runs the weekly session. The streak shows you, at a glance, which teams kept the habit |
| **Tells the truth about health** | A goal nobody checked in on shows as outdated, whatever it last claimed. Divergence between reported health and the numbers is flagged |
| **Prepares your review** | Before the quarterly review, the pack is ready: scores to confirm, misses awaiting a cause, retro prompts, the process-health survey and a minutes skeleton |

## 6. Shape the method

## Think a rule is wrong? Change it for everyone.

OpenOKR's method is a public document. Every rule names its published source. If your experience says a threshold, a check or an agenda is wrong, open an issue with your reasoning. If you are right, the product changes, and your name is on the change.

We would rather be argued with by practitioners than agreed with by nobody.
