## 2. Two different jobs

## KPIs keep the lights on. OKRs change what they show.

| KPIs | OKRs |
|---|---|
| A number you watch every period, whether or not anybody is trying to change it | A deliberate change, for a quarter or a year |
| Describe the health of the business | Describe what you are changing about it |
| Live as long as the business does | Close, get scored, and feed the next cycle |

**Line under the table:** They connect in three ways. A key result can be measured by a live KPI. An unhealthy KPI can start a recovery OKR. And every KPI's baseline is an input to planning.

## 3. Driver trees

## Find the branch that is failing, then the lever at its edge.

A tree has one root, usually a lagging number such as revenue or operating margin. Its leaves are leading drivers a team can act on this week. A link is either a formula, where the child is part of the parent's calculation, or an influence, where the child is believed to move the parent.

**Diagram:**

```
Operating margin                          unhealthy
├── Revenue per account                   healthy
└── Support cost per account              unhealthy
    ├── Tickets per account               watch      ← leading
    └── Self-serve answer rate            unhealthy  ← leading
```

**Caption:** To move the root, find the unhealthy branch. The leading drivers at its edge are your candidate key results.

## 4. Health bands

## Every KPI in its own units.

Set green and red boundaries in the KPI's own units: stay at or above, stay at or below, increase to, decrease to, or stay within a range. No boundaries? It falls back to how close the reading is to its target.

| Healthy | Watch | Unhealthy | No data |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inside the green boundary | Between the boundaries. Watch the leading drivers | Past the red boundary. Decide a response | Enter a value and a target |

**Line:** A KPI under recovery is shown as **recovering** beside its real band, never instead of it. You can see the fix working without anyone mistaking it for the number having recovered.

## 5. Three responses

## An unhealthy KPI asks for a decision.

**Three cards:**

| Fix it now | Add a key result | Launch a recovery OKR |
|---|---|---|
| Treat it as day-to-day work, with an owner and a date | Put it under an objective you already have | A committed objective, drafted for you, to restore a level the business relies on |

## 6. The recovery OKR, drafted

## Drafted from the drivers that are short.

The draft is ready the moment the KPI turns unhealthy. The Champion proposes it after two unhealthy periods in a row, or at once if the KPI falls straight from healthy to unhealthy.

**Annotated draft:**

```
Objective   Margins we can run the business on again          committed
            The owner writes the why.

KR 1        Operating margin from 9.1% to 15%                 the KPI itself
KR 2        Self-serve answer rate from 22% to 40%            Mei Lin
KR 3        Tickets per account from 3.4 to 2.5               Kofi Asante
```

**Callouts:**

1. **The objective** names what the KPI protects, in words, not numbers.
2. **The first key result** is the KPI itself, from where it is to where it is healthy.
3. **Up to three more**, one per leading driver at the edge of the failing branch that is below its own target, each owned by whoever owns that driver.
4. **Drivers already on target are skipped.**

When the reading is healthy again, the Champion proposes closing the recovery OKR.

**Small print:** The draft is built by rules, so it works with the AI switched off. With an AI provider connected, the Coach adds a plain-language reading of the trend.
