# Messaging

What we say, in what order, and how it sounds. Every page in [pages/](pages/) is written from this file. The numbers it quotes are in [claims.md](claims.md).

## 1. The three names

Settled with the maintainer on 1 November 2026.

| Name | What it is | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|
| **OKR Institute** | The company. It has coached, trained and certified OKR practitioners since 2017, and it makes okri.io, its earlier OKR platform. The "we" in every line of copy | The footer, About, Enterprise, support, partners, legal |
| **OKRGoal** | The cloud product: OpenOKR, run for you by OKR Institute. What a visitor to okrgoal.com signs up for | The logo, the nav, every product and feature page, pricing |
| **OpenOKR** | The open source product, under AGPL-3.0. The same release OKRGoal runs, for organisations that host it themselves | Self-host, the licence, GitHub, METHOD.md, Community, Security |

**The rules:**

1. **Feature copy names OKRGoal.** "OKRGoal checks every line as you type." It is the product the reader can sign up for.
2. **Open source copy names OpenOKR.** When the subject is self-hosting, the licence, the code, GitHub or METHOD.md, the name is OpenOKR.
3. **"We" is OKR Institute.** "We run it for you." "Nobody at OKR Institute can open your workspace."
4. **Say how they relate** the first time a page uses two of the names:

   > OKRGoal is OpenOKR, run for you by OKR Institute. OpenOKR is open source, so you can also run it yourself, free.

5. **Credit the lessons,** where a page needs it, in these words or close to them: *Built by OKR Institute, on what it learned running okri.io and coaching organisations through OKRs since 2017.* Go no further, for example by naming an OKR Institute framework as the method, unless the maintainers decide to.
6. **Never mention a business connection to any other OKR software company.**

The model is WordPress.com and WordPress, or n8n Cloud and n8n: one release, a hosted product and an open source one, from one maker.

## 2. Positioning

**For** organisations that want OKRs to work, not just to be written down,
**OKRGoal is** the OKR platform your organisation runs its OKRs on, with the method built in and two AI teammates that act, from OKR Institute and open source at its core.
**Unlike** OKR trackers, which store objectives and wait to be visited,
**it** checks every objective as it is written, runs the weekly and quarterly rhythm, and chases what is late, in the browser and in the chat apps people already use.
**And because** it runs on open source OpenOKR, you can read every rule it enforces, change it, run it on your own servers, and switch the AI off without losing any of it.

### What OKRGoal is, and what it is not

OKRGoal is the software an organisation runs its real OKRs on: its people and teams, its cycles, its objectives, key results and KPIs, its weekly check-ins and its quarterly reviews. **It is not a course, a training tool or a place to practise OKRs.** A visitor must never leave a page thinking they are buying training.

Two things on the site pull the other way, so handle them on purpose:

| What pulls toward "training" | How to handle it |
|---|---|
| The word **practice**. The product's documents mean the discipline an organisation carries out, as in "medical practice". A skimming reader hears rehearsal | Never put "practice" in a headline, a page title or a meta description. In body copy say **the method** for the rules and **your OKRs** for what the organisation runs. "Practice settings" stays, because it is the product's own name for a screen, and "published practice" becomes "published sources". Never write "practise" |
| **OKR Institute's record** (courses, people trained, certification) and the word **Coach** | The record is the maker's credentials, not what the product does. Say "built by". Keep training and certification on About, Enterprise, Resources and the footer, as OKR Institute's, never as a feature of OKRGoal. The Coach is a feature that checks your real drafts. It is never the product's identity, so it never leads the home page |

The test for any line: **would someone skimming it think they are buying a course?** If yes, rewrite it around their organisation's OKRs.

People do learn the method while they use OKRGoal, because every message explains its rule. Say that where it helps, as a side effect of doing the real work, never as the reason to buy.

### The one-liner

**Stop storing OKRs. Start running them.**

It says what the product is for in six words: running an organisation's OKRs. The contrast with storing is the difference from every tracker, and it is short enough for a browser tab, a social card and a GitHub description. It is the product's own line (REQUIREMENTS.md §1).

**Retired on 1 November 2026: "Your OKR coach, built in."** It made the coach the product, and read as a training tool. The Coach is still a headline feature on the agents page, under a page that has already said what OKRGoal is.

### The paragraph

> Most organisations do not fail at OKRs because of the tool. They fail because the rhythm stops. OKRGoal is where your organisation's OKRs live and run. It puts the method in the product and makes the product active. An AI Coach checks every objective and key result as you write it. An AI Champion keeps the weekly rhythm, in Slack, Teams, WhatsApp, Telegram and email. Every message cites the rule behind it, so you can argue with it. Turn the AI off and every rule still runs. It runs on OpenOKR, which is open source, so nothing is gated and you can run it yourself or let us run it. It is built by OKR Institute, on what it has learned coaching organisations through OKRs since 2017.

## 5. The voice

OpenOKR's own documents already have a voice, and it is better than borrowing one. It is dry, specific, and a little stubborn. The reference sites (see [reference/](reference/README.md)) add confidence and pace. Together:

| Principle | Sounds like | Never sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **Name the real failure** | "Somebody wrote objectives in a spreadsheet in January, and nobody checked in by March." | "Unlock alignment across your organisation." |
| **Short and declarative** | "It comes to you." "Nothing is gated." | "Our innovative platform empowers teams to achieve more." |
| **Specific beats impressive** | "A key result with a target but no baseline cannot prove movement." | "Advanced AI-powered quality insights." |
| **Say the honest limit** | "Deliberately OKR-shaped. Not a project-management suite." | "Everything you need, all in one place." |
| **Opinion, with a reason** | "A plan where nothing was cut is a plan that has not been made." | "Best practices built in." |
| **Wry, never jokey** | "Nothing keeps it going on a wet Tuesday in week six." | Puns, emoji in headlines, exclamation marks |
| **Respect the reader** | "You can open the rule and disagree with it." | "Don't worry, our AI handles everything!" |

### Mechanics

- Plain English. Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- **No em dashes.** Use a full stop, a comma or a colon. This is the repository's rule and the site follows it.
- British spelling, to match the product: organisation, prioritise, colour.
- Second person for the reader ("you", "your team"). First person plural for OKR Institute ("we run it"), only where a person would say it.
- Numbers as words in body copy up to twenty-six when they are product facts ("twenty-six checks", "eight phases"), as figures in stats rows and tables ("26", "8"). Prices always as figures.
- Headlines in sentence case. No full stop on a headline unless it is two sentences ("Turn the AI off. It still coaches.").
- Name the agents with capitals: **the OKR Coach**, **the OKR Champion**. After the first use on a page, "the Coach" and "the Champion".
- A champion (lower case) is the person who owns a goal. When both appear close together, say "the goal's champion" and "the Champion agent".
- **"Practice" stays out of headlines, page titles and meta descriptions.** In body copy, "the method" for the rules and "your OKRs" for what the organisation runs. "Practice settings" is the product's own term and stays. See "What OKRGoal is, and what it is not" in §2.

## 7. Words

| Use | Instead of | Why |
|---|---|---|
| the method, the rules, the rhythm | best practices, framework, "the practice" | It is a specific, written method. Say so. "Practice" reads as rehearsal to a skimming reader |
| your OKRs, your organisation's OKRs | the practice (for what the organisation runs) | It says the product is where the real work happens |
| OKR platform, OKR software | OKR training, OKR course, learn OKRs (about the product) | Training is OKR Institute's, sold beside the product. The product is where OKRs run |
| practice settings | (keep it) | The product's own name for the settings screen |
| AI teammate, agent | bot, assistant, copilot (for the Coach and Champion) | They are members with names and a scope. "Copilot" is a different feature in the product |
| nudge, escalate, chase | notify, alert | It says who acts |
| check-in | update, status report | The product's own word |
| self-host | on-premise, on-prem | The product's own word. Say "on your own servers" once on pages for buyers who use "on-prem" |
| open source | open-core | Nothing is held back, so "open core" would be wrong |
| nothing is gated | free features, unlimited | Precise, and it is the promise |
| proposes, you approve | automates, does it for you | The agents propose by default |

**Never on the site:** unlock, empower, leverage, seamless, game-changing, revolutionary, supercharge, synergy, "AI-powered" as a headline adjective, "simply", "just" (as in "just click"), "practise", and exclamation marks.
